I wasn't a particularly athletic child.

Physical education in elementary school seemed to revolve almost entirely around football, a sport I have never managed to enjoy.

It wasn't until middle and high school that I began exploring other sports and discovering that physical activity could actually be enjoyable.

My relationship with my body has been complicated for much of my life. I've always been on the heavier side, and for a long time, my weight influenced how I saw myself.

Today, strength training and running are important parts of my life.

They give me a sense of agency over my body, but more importantly, they give me somewhere to exist outside my professional identity.

At the gym, I don't have to think about patients, research, deadlines, or career plans. On a long run, I can simply move, look around, and enjoy being somewhere.

Not every moment needs to be productive.

I've completed half marathons, and one of my current ambitions is to finish my first full marathon. I'm not entirely sure why running 42.195 kilometres appeals to me so much. Perhaps I want to prove something to myself.

Running has also taught me something that extends well beyond exercise: everyone has their own pace.

It's a lesson I occasionally need to remind myself of when I'm comparing my career, progress, or life with someone else's.
